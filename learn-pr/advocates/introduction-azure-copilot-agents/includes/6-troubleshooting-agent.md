Use the [Azure Copilot Troubleshooting agent](/azure/copilot/troubleshooting-agent) when you have a symptom to investigate on a specific Azure resource. For example, if checkout stopped responding when a virtual machine (VM) restarted, start with that VM.

## Diagnose a resource problem

1. In the Azure portal, open **Support + Troubleshooting** on the affected resource. Alternatively, select **Azure Copilot > New chat > Troubleshooting** and identify the resource and subscription.
1. Describe what happened and when. For example, ask, "Why did checkout-vm restart at 14:05 UTC?" If the Observability agent identified an event, share its timestamp and findings in the chat.
1. Compare the agent's explanation with the evidence for 14:05 UTC. Look for a restart operation in the VM's **Activity log** and a platform event in **Resource health**. Ask the VM operator to check the [guest system logs for a restart initiated inside the VM](/troubleshoot/azure/virtual-machines/windows/understand-vm-reboot#user-initiated-reboot-or-shutdown-actions). An [empty Activity log doesn't rule out downtime](/troubleshoot/azure/virtual-machines/windows/understand-vm-reboot#missing-vm-downtimes-in-activity-log).
1. If those sources don't establish a cause, follow the suggested diagnostic steps or self-help article. What the agent can diagnose depends on the service, your permissions, and the available data. Don't delay an urgent escalation to obtain guest logs.
1. For some issues, the agent offers a one-click fix. Review the proposed change and its impact. Confirm only if you're authorized to make it, then verify that checkout responds again.

## Escalate an unresolved issue

If the problem persists, ask the agent to [prepare a support request](/azure/copilot/troubleshooting-agent#get-started). It can prefill the description with session details and diagnostic results. Preparing the request doesn't open a case with Microsoft Support.

1. Check the [support plan and permission requirements](/azure/azure-portal/supportability/how-to-create-azure-support-request). To file a request for a subscription, you need a role such as **Support Request Contributor** on that subscription. Technical issues also require a plan that includes technical support; billing and subscription management questions don't require a paid plan.
1. Review the subscription, affected resource, and description. Include the time and time zone, user impact, and results of any fixes. For the restart, summarize what each available source showed. If the cause is unknown, say so rather than blaming Azure or the app. Remove secrets, personal information, and confidential details from the description and attachments. Use the designated contact fields for your contact details.
1. Under [**Advanced diagnostic information**](/azure/azure-portal/supportability/how-to-create-azure-support-request#additional-details), separately choose whether Microsoft Support may collect the offered diagnostics. Follow your organization's policy; attaching a log isn't consent to further collection.
1. For some VM problem types, you can separately allow or decline [access to VM memory](/azure/azure-portal/supportability/how-to-create-azure-support-request#memory-dump-collection). A memory dump can contain sensitive data. If Support collects it, the VM can pause for **up to 10 minutes** and then resume without restarting. Before consenting for the checkout VM, confirm that your data-handling policy permits collection and that the workload owner approves the interruption.
1. Select an available **Severity** that matches the business impact. Check the support plan, country/region, contact method, and who will answer. If checkout has critical business impact and you choose [**Severity A**](https://azure.microsoft.com/support/plans/response/), your team commits to working with Microsoft 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Name a reachable incident contact; Microsoft may lower the severity if your team can't sustain that coverage. A target for the initial response isn't a promise of when checkout will be restored.
1. On **Review + create**, select **Create** to submit the request, then keep the case number to track it.

If the restart might have been platform-initiated and the cause is still unknown, ask the next responder to recheck [Resource health](/troubleshoot/azure/virtual-machines/windows/understand-vm-reboot#resource-health-information). Azure may post additional details up to 72 hours after the initial unavailability. Don't delay an urgent request while waiting for a possible update. Add any new evidence to the existing case.
