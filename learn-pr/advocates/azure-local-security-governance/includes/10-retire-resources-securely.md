Security responsibilities continue when a workload, node, or cluster is retired. Deleting its Azure representation doesn't erase its local disks, remove every credential, or prove that retained backups remain recoverable. *Media sanitization* is the approved process for making stored data unrecoverable at the assurance level required for that media and its future use.

Use the following retirement sequence:

1. **Approve scope and retention.** Identify the workload or hardware being retired, its owner, remaining consumers, required clinical records, evidence retention, and any legal hold. Record which data and recovery material must remain and when disposal may occur.
1. **Preserve recovery dependencies.** Confirm that retained backups have the required key versions, wrapping keys, authorization path, and usable restore destination. Keep the corresponding protected repository and recovery identity available until those dependencies end. Removing a VM doesn't make its old backups independent of their keys.
1. **Remove the resource through its supported lifecycle.** Distinguish workload deletion, removal of a node from a surviving cluster, and retirement of an entire instance. Confirm that the remaining topology is supported and has sufficient capacity and health before an approved node removal. Coordinate local platform operations with Azure and Arc resource cleanup rather than deleting only the cloud record.
1. **Remove obsolete authority and connections.** Review role assignments, local and application accounts, service-principal credentials, managed-identity permissions, certificates, network rules, and monitoring registrations. Remove only access that no surviving service or retained recovery process needs. Don't delete a shared witness, vault, identity, or repository because one consumer has been retired.
1. **Sanitize released media and management state.** Use an approved, media-appropriate clearing, purging, or destruction process compatible with the hardware and disposal policy. Don't sanitize media subject to a legal or investigation hold until the authorized owner releases it, even if an evidence export already exists. Disk encryption, a deleted partition, or a reset TPM alone isn't proof of sanitization. Include removable media and relevant BMC configuration or credentials in the hardware handover review.
1. **Demonstrate closure.** Retain the asset identifiers, approvals, sanitization evidence, custody or disposal record, removed-access results, and remaining retention obligations. Confirm that required recovery and evidence repositories still work and that retired access paths no longer do.

For Contoso, the platform team can complete hardware removal while the recovery and records owners continue to govern retained patient data, backups, and logs. Close those obligations separately. Destroy the final required key only when all dependent retention and recovery obligations permit it.
