Digital sovereignty means making self-determined, evidence-based choices about data, infrastructure, and operations. Requirements vary by workload, jurisdiction, sensitivity, and criticality.

In this module, you learned to:

- Explain why digital sovereignty matters for compliance, risk management, public trust, and continuity.
- Describe data controls, operational controls, and technological independence.
- Compare Sovereign Public Cloud, Sovereign Private Cloud, and National Partner Clouds within the broader Sovereign Partner Ecosystem.
- Map a global baseline, regional commitments, and country/region-specific implementation patterns to workload needs.
- Explain Microsoft's five European digital commitments and distinguish the EU Data Boundary's scoped commitments from absolute residency claims.
- Use risk and resilience questions to assess evidence, choose mitigations, assign accountability, and review residual risk.

Sovereignty isn't a one-time deployment decision. Verify current service availability, configuration requirements, contractual scope, and exceptions; implement and test the controls; then reassess as requirements change.
