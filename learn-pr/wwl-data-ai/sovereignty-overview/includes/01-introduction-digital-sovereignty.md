Organizations want to innovate while protecting sensitive data and maintaining the trust of citizens and customers. Governments and regulated industries must also respond to changing requirements for AI, cybersecurity, privacy, and the continuity of essential services.

Digital transformation can help organizations:

- Modernize public and private sector services.
- Digitize manual processes.
- Improve experiences for citizens, customers, and employees.
- Create opportunities for social and economic growth.

Cloud computing provides scale, agility, and access to new capabilities. However, organizations need to understand how cloud services meet local requirements, where data is stored and processed, and who can access it. Digital sovereignty helps frame these decisions.

## What is digital sovereignty?

**Digital sovereignty** is the ability of organizations and governments to participate securely and independently in the digital economy with self-determined controls over their data, infrastructure, and operations. It isn't necessarily isolation. It means making deliberate governance choices in a connected digital environment.

Data security, regulatory standards, technology dependencies, global trade, and geopolitical conditions shape the sovereignty landscape. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, industry, and workload.

For example, a public information website and a system that processes sensitive citizen records might belong to the same agency but need different sovereignty controls.

> [!TIP]
> Selecting an appropriate cloud model starts with those workload requirements, not with a single deployment choice for the entire organization.

## Why does sovereignty matter?

Sovereignty requirements affect four areas of cloud planning:

- **Legal compliance:** Organizations must identify applicable requirements for data storage, processing, access, and transfers.
- **Risk management:** Controls can reduce exposure to unauthorized access, geopolitical risk, and operational disruption.
- **Public trust:** Governments and critical industries need to demonstrate how they protect sensitive workloads and maintain accountability.
- **Continuity and operational independence:** Essential services might need to operate through changes in connectivity, security, or external operating conditions.

These priorities help you identify the controls needed for each workload.

## Microsoft's approach

Microsoft's approach treats sovereignty as a capability to build into cloud adoption, rather than a requirement to use one isolated environment. Three ideas guide this approach:

- **Available broadly:** Microsoft cloud services provide a foundation of security, privacy, and compliance capabilities. Individual sovereignty features still have service, region, licensing, or availability requirements.
- **Well-governed controls:** Technical, contractual, and operational measures work together. A contract alone doesn't configure a workload, and a technical control alone doesn't establish legal compliance.
- **Workload-dependent choices:** Sensitivity, criticality, and required operating authority determine the controls for each workload.

Microsoft Sovereign Cloud brings together public cloud, customer-operated private cloud, and partner capabilities to help organizations make these choices.

In this module, you learn to explain sovereignty controls, compare cloud models for common scenarios, map country/region and European commitments to requirements, and use evidence-based risk and resilience questions to guide decisions.
