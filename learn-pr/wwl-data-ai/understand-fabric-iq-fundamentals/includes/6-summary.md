The installation-readiness question starts with separate project, equipment, and maintenance records. Microsoft Fabric IQ helps connect those records through shared business concepts, so teams and supported agents use consistent definitions when they investigate a dependency.

In this module, you learned how Fabric IQ fits within Microsoft IQ and how its build-bind-query workflow connects business meaning to prepared data. You distinguished entity types from instances and connected properties, relationships, and rules to the business questions they support.

You can now distinguish trusted metrics and reporting from relationship exploration, read-only questions, configured monitoring, and planning. An ontology complements a Power BI semantic model rather than replacing it. Graph materialization is an optional choice for eligible data when dependency paths matter, not a prerequisite for every question.

You also compared report-specific analytical schemas with concept-driven ontology modeling. Starting with shared concepts makes definitions reusable across consumers instead of requiring each team to reinterpret source columns and relationships.

In the offshore construction example, task T001 is at risk because maintenance order M001 blocks its required crane E001 during the planned installation window. Shared definitions identify the dependency; explicit overlap checks and current source or graph data make the conclusion useful. You can recognize when an ontology provides that shared context while keeping data preparation, query validation, and operational judgment separate.
