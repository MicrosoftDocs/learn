An application that serves customers on three continents inherits a problem that a single-region database never has to answer: what happens when one region stops responding. The database keeps running everywhere else, but only if someone decided in advance which regions hold the data, which region accepts writes, and what the application does the moment its nearest region goes quiet. Those decisions are made when the account is configured, not when the outage starts.

Contoso runs a product catalog on Azure Cosmos DB for NoSQL. The account lives in one region, and the read latency from Europe and Asia is high enough that the storefront feels slow to most of the customer base. A recent regional incident elsewhere in the business also made the operations team ask a question nobody could answer: if the catalog's region went offline, how long would the storefront be down, and how much recent data would be gone. You're the developer who has to turn those two concerns into an account configuration and a client configuration that hold up under a real outage.

This module works through that configuration in order. You start with how Azure Cosmos DB replicates data and what each replication choice costs in money and in recovery objectives. You add regions and route reads to the closest one from the SDK. You enable writes in more than one region and handle the conflicts that follow. You then compare the failover operations the service offers, from a full account failover down to failover of a single partition, and rehearse one against a live account.

By the end of this module, you can configure an Azure Cosmos DB account and its clients for multi-region availability and choose the failover behavior that matches an application's recovery objectives.
