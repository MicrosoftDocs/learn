## Supported employment

A useful way to assess any tool is to ask whether it helps the employee and or the employer succeed, build skills, exercise choice, and participate fully in the workplace. A tool should remove barriers, not create new ones, or make the employee dependent on a system that does not fit their needs.

## The job coach role

Job coaching includes preparation, instruction, coordination, employer partnership, in-work support, and follow-through. AI may help draft a checklist, organize non-identifying notes, or create options for a conversation. It should not decide what an employee wants, whether a person can succeed, or which support is right.

> Decision ownership: AI can help draft materials. The job coach and employee decide what is accurate, useful, and appropriate. They work together to identify the right support and how those supports will be used.

## Values that should guide the work

| **Value** | **What it means for AI use** |
|---|---|
| **Dignity** | Treat the employee as a worker and adult. Use respectful language and support workplace belonging. |
| **Self-determination** | The employee directs decisions about work and support. Provide information and support needed for informed choice. |
| **Presumed competence** | Start from the belief that the employee can learn and contribute. Use support to remove barriers, not lower expectations. |
| **Confidentiality** | Use the least amount of information needed and follow organizational policy. |
| **Transparency and consent** | Tell the employee when AI is used to create material about them or for them. Invite participation, respect a decision to decline, and confirm how information may be used. |

> Partnership: Employers must be valued as an equal partner. When AI is being used to break down barriers in the workplace, it is important both the employee and employer are in agreement.
