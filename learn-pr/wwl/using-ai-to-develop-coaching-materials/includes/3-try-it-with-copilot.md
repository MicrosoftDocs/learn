Each material below follows the same pattern. Give Copilot verified, non-identifying information, then review and adapt the draft with the employee and the employer. Copy a prompt, replace the bracketed details, and treat the result as a starting point rather than a finished product.

## Task analysis

**Prompt**

> Using the verified employer instructions below, draft a numbered task analysis for [task]. Use short sentences and one action per step. Keep every safety and quality requirement. Mark any missing or uncertain information instead of guessing. No personal details.

- Review whether the sequence matches the worksite.
- Ask the employee what format, wording, photographs, or graphics would help.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Don't use AI translation for safety-critical instructions unless a competent speaker verifies the complete translated version.

## Employee-approved profile or summary

AI can reorganize or simplify a profile that the employee and job coach have already developed. It shouldn't turn keywords or raw notes into a vocational profile, speak in the employee's voice, or decide what the employee wants shared.

**Prompt**

> Reorganize the employee-approved content below into a one-page summary. Keep the meaning. Lead with strengths, interests, contributions, and conditions for success. Don't add information or write in first person. Mark any unclear points for review.

## Prompting and support plan

**Prompt**

> Using the approved task analysis below, organize a draft support plan. For each step, list possible visual, gestural, verbal, coworker, supervisor, or environmental supports. Begin with the least intrusive support and include a space for the coach to record what works. Don't decide which support to use.

## Workplace instructions

**Prompt**

> Rewrite the verified instructions below using familiar words, short sentences, and one idea per line. Keep every safety and quality requirement. Add space for a photograph or graphic after each step. Use only the information provided.

## Practice scenarios

**Prompt**

> Draft three short workplace scenarios for practicing [conversation]. Include what's happening and several response options. Use respectful adult language. Don't assume how the employee communicates.
