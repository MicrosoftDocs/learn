AI is moving beyond experiences that simply answer questions or generate content. AI agents can use connected tools and applications to perform tasks on a user’s behalf. For example, a sales employee might ask an agent to update a customer account. The agent can retrieve the customer's information, update the account record in a business application, and create a follow-up task, reducing the manual effort required to complete the process.

This ability to take action changes the enterprise attack surface. Traditional security strategies already account for users, applications, devices, and data. With AI agents, organizations also need to consider what software can access those resources and what it can do with that access.

## From access to action

Consider the difference between these two scenarios:

- An AI experience provides a user with information about a customer.
- An AI agent retrieves the customer's information and then updates a record in a business application.

The difference is that the agent isn't only providing information. It can also take action within business systems.

As organizations move from individual AI experiences toward agents operating across business workflows, they need a governance approach that accounts for these actions.
