You're working through your morning when a message arrives from your manager.

A leadership review has moved up unexpectedly, and you need to provide a quick project status briefing in 15 minutes. To prepare, you need to gather information from several sources, review recent project materials, and organize your workspace so the most important resources are easy to access.

Instead of manually searching through applications and windows, you use Voice Access to quickly locate the information you need and prepare for the discussion.

## Your challenge

Before the leadership review begins, complete the following tasks:

- Use Voice Access to open a communication or collaboration application.
- Open a document containing project status information.
- Locate supporting resources, such as customer feedback, planning documents, or meeting notes.
- Move between open applications using voice commands.
- Organize your workspace so key information is readily available during the briefing.

## Why this matters

Information workers often need to respond to unexpected requests with little time to prepare.

Before you can answer questions, make recommendations, or share updates, you first need to find the information that supports those conversations. Searching for documents, switching between applications, and getting your workspace ready can take valuable time away from analyzing information and making decisions.

Voice Access can help simplify these navigation tasks, giving you more time to review the available information and prepare for the discussion.

## Reflection

After completing this exercise, consider the following questions:

- How quickly were you able to locate the information you needed?
- Which navigation tasks benefited most from voice commands?
- What workspace setup would help you stay focused during a high-priority discussion?

## Key takeaway

Voice Access helps you quickly find information, move between applications, and organize your workspace. These navigation skills can help you spend less time searching for resources and more time preparing for the work that depends on them.
