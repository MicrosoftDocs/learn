As you work through the takeaways from a day full of meetings, a pattern begins to emerge. Customers consistently mention the same challenge, and several recent support requests suggest there's an opportunity to improve the experience.

Your manager asks for a recommendation before the next planning session.

Rather than planning out how to structure a document or write the perfect opening sentence, you use Fluid Dictation to capture your thoughts, develop your ideas, and create a recommendation that informs future decisions in less time.

## Exercise objectives

Use Fluid Dictation to:

- Capture and organize your initial ideas.
- Summarize a customer problem or opportunity.
- Develop a recommendation based on available information.
- Create a draft proposal or planning document.
- Review and refine your content before sharing it.

## Your challenge

Imagine you're presenting an improvement proposal to stakeholders.

Your recommendation should address several key questions:

- What opportunity or challenge have you identified?
- Why does it matter?
- What evidence supports your recommendation?
- What action should the team take next?

Take a moment to imagine some answers to these questions, then use Fluid Dictation to record them. Begin by speaking naturally, as if you were discussing the idea with a colleague.

Focus on getting your thoughts out of your head and onto the page. Don't worry about creating a polished document immediately. Use Fluid Dictation to capture the major themes, supporting rationale, and recommended actions.

Once your ideas are captured, review the draft and refine it so stakeholders can quickly understand the recommendation and its potential value.

## Why this matters

Before a proposal becomes a presentation, or a recommendation becomes a project plan, someone must develop the idea behind it.

For information workers, one of the biggest points of friction is often moving from ideation to creation. Valuable insights, observations, and recommendations can be lost when structure, wording, or formatting dominate their attention.

Fluid Dictation helps you focus on capturing ideas first so you can build momentum, preserve context, and spend more time refining your message instead of deciding what to say in the first place.

## Reflection

After completing this exercise, consider the following questions:

- How did speaking your ideas differ from typing them?
- Which parts of the recommendation were easiest to develop through dictation?
- How could Fluid Dictation help you during other planning, brainstorming, and decision-making activities?

## Key takeaway

Fluid Dictation helps you move quickly from ideas to drafted content. By capturing thoughts as they emerge, you can spend less time with a blank page and more time developing recommendations, plans, and communications that move work forward.
