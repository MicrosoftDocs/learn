Imagine that you're looking to increase your productivity at work but have difficulty using a traditional mouse and keyboard. You have limited mobility. Limited mobility can range from not being able to perform movement with the arms and legs—like walking or reaching for things—and having difficulty using a traditional mouse, keyboard, pen, or touchscreen. It can also include those born with permanent disabilities, someone who had an accident, or experienced a trauma, and individuals aging into disability. Let's review some examples of the barriers faced by those with limited mobility:

- **Limited manipulation:** Functional challenges with things like grasp, coordination, fine motor skills, and speed.
- **Limited reach:** Functional challenges with things like coordination, muscle tone, and posture.
- **Limited strength:** Functional challenges with things like endurance, control, and muscle tone.

Microsoft Adaptive Accessories help address this barrier by giving users more flexible input options. The ecosystem includes the Microsoft Adaptive Mouse, Microsoft Adaptive Hub, and Microsoft Adaptive Buttons, along with compatible accessories and customizable components sold separately. These tools can be used individually or together to replace or augment traditional mouse and keyboard input. With these accessories business users can build an input experience that matches how they move, reach, press, and work.

**Why this matters:** Accessibility settings and adaptive hardware are most effective when learners can connect them to real work. By understanding the barrier first, learners can recommend or configure tools that support independence, reduce fatigue, and help employees complete daily tasks in a way that works for them.

**Use case scenario:** A team member works across Microsoft 365 apps throughout the day but finds repeated mouse movements and keyboard shortcuts tiring. By pairing adaptive input options with their Surface device, they can create a setup that reduces repetitive movement and supports a more comfortable work routine.

## Microsoft Adaptive Mouse

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/adaptive-mouse.png" alt-text="Photo of the Microsoft Adaptive Mouse on a plain white background without any attachment.":::

When a traditional mouse is difficult to hold, move, or click, pointer control can become a daily productivity barrier. The Microsoft Adaptive Mouse helps address this by offering a small, lightweight mouse that can be used on its own or customized with a Microsoft Adaptive Mouse Tail and Thumb Support, sold separately. For users who need a different shape or grip, the mouse can also support more customization through 3D-printable designs. The workplace outcome is reliable pointer control for selecting content, navigating apps, managing files, and completing everyday tasks.

**Use case scenario:** A user can navigate apps but has difficulty maintaining a comfortable grip on a standard mouse. The Adaptive Mouse, combined with a mouse tail or thumb support when needed, can help the user build a pointing experience that matches their hand position and movement range.

Before connecting the Adaptive Mouse, review the main components so you can identify the controls you'll use during setup:

- USB-C charging port.
- Power button, eject button, and Bluetooth pairing button on the bottom of the Adaptive Mouse (not shown).
- Mouse tail cover.

After you review the hardware, connect the Adaptive Mouse to your PC so you can begin using it as part of a personalized input setup:

1. Remove the Adaptive Mouse from its packaging.
2. Press the power button on your Adaptive Mouse.
3. On your PC, a notification appears. It asks you to connect the mouse to your PC with Bluetooth. Select **Connect**.

>[!NOTE]
> If you don't see the notification, select **Start > Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Add a device > Bluetooth**. Select **Microsoft Adaptive Mouse** from the list.

You can further personalize the Microsoft Adaptive Mouse by attaching the Microsoft Adaptive Mouse Tail and Microsoft Adaptive Mouse Thumb Support, both sold separately. These accessories can help create a more stable or comfortable grip based on the user's needs.

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/adaptive-mouse-tail.png" alt-text="Photo of the Microsoft Adaptive Mouse Tail attachment on a white background not connected to Microsoft Adaptive Mouse.":::

To attach the Mouse Tail, do the following:

1. Flip the mouse over so that the bottom is facing up.
2. Press and hold the Eject button to loosen the mouse tail cover, then pull it away from the mouse.
3. Slide the mouse tail into the same location as the battery cover.

To remove the mouse tail, follow these instructions again.

Included with the Adaptive Mouse Tail is the Adaptive Mouse Thumb Support. Here's how to attach the thumb support:

1. Make sure the thumb support is on the side of the mouse where you want your thumb to rest.
2. Align the two location markers on the thumb support with the slots on your mouse.
3. Press the thumb support into place to secure it to your mouse.

To remove the thumb support, do the following:

1. Firmly hold the mouse in one hand with the thumb support pointed away from you.
2. Push the side facing you away from the mouse—the location marker should be lifted away from the slot on the mouse.
3. Pull the side with the thumb support away from the mouse.

## Microsoft Adaptive Hub

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/microsoft-adaptive-hub.png" alt-text="Photo of Microsoft Adaptive Hub over a white background.":::

Some users need input methods that go beyond a traditional keyboard. The Microsoft Adaptive Hub helps address this barrier by acting as a central connection point for custom inputs. It can replace or augment keyboard interactions by connecting to Microsoft Adaptive Buttons, compatible switches, and other assistive technology. The Hub can connect to up to three devices wirelessly or by USB-C, and it can connect wirelessly to up to four Microsoft Adaptive Buttons, sold separately.

**Use case scenario:** A user frequently switches between communication, scheduling, and document tasks but finds repeated keyboard shortcuts difficult to complete consistently. The Adaptive Hub can help by connecting adaptive buttons or switches that trigger common actions through customized profiles.

Before connecting the Adaptive Hub, review its main controls and ports so you can understand how it supports device pairing, profile switching, charging, and assistive technology connections:

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/adaptive-hub-diagram-front.png" alt-text="Diagram of the front of the Microsoft Adaptive Hub showing a variety of port selections.":::

1. **Power button:** Turn your Adaptive Hub on and off.
2. **Bluetooth button:** Switch or pair to another PC or device when you press this button.
3. **Bluetooth LED (3):** The LED tells you which device is connected.
4. **Profile LED (3):** The LED tells you which profile is selected.
5. **Profile button:** Use this to switch between Profiles on your Adaptive Hub.
6. **Pairing button:** This button pairs your Adaptive Hub to any of the adaptive buttons.
7. **USB-C ports (3):** Connect to Microsoft adaptive buttons with a USB-C cable. When your Adaptive Hub is connected to power, these ports will also charge your buttons.

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/adaptive-hub-diagram-back.png" alt-text="Diagram of the back of the Microsoft Adaptive Hub showing a variety of port selections.":::

1. **USB-C charging port:** Connect this port to a power-source with a USB-C cable to charge your Adaptive Hub.
2. **3.5 mm port (5):** Use this port to connect to other assistive tech switches.

After you review the hardware, connect the Adaptive Hub to your Surface device so it can act as the control center for adaptive buttons, switches, and custom input profiles:

1. Press the power button on your Adaptive Hub.
2. On your PC, a notification appears. It asks you to connect the Adaptive Hub to your Surface with Bluetooth. Select **Connect**.

>[!NOTE]
> If you don't see the notification, select **Start > Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Add a device > Bluetooth**. Select **Microsoft Adaptive Hub** from the list.

To charge your Adaptive Hub, follow these instructions:

1. Connect the included USB-C cable to the USB-C port on the shortest side of your Adaptive Hub.
2. Connect the other end of the charging cable to power, like a USB port.

## Microsoft Adaptive Buttons

Repetitive keyboard commands, small keys, or complex input sequences can be barriers for users with limited mobility, strength, or dexterity. Microsoft Adaptive Buttons help address this barrier by providing programmable wireless buttons that work with the Microsoft Adaptive Hub. Users can choose from button types such as a D-pad, joystick, or dual button, each sold separately. Each button can support multiple digital inputs and can be configured to perform actions that match the user's workflow.

**Use case scenario:** A user regularly needs to mute calls, open frequently used apps, or trigger keyboard shortcuts, but completing those actions through a standard keyboard slows them down. An Adaptive Button connected through the Adaptive Hub can provide a single, easier-to-reach input for those repeated tasks.

After choosing the right button type, pair it with the Adaptive Hub so the button can be configured for the user's preferred actions:

1. Make sure your Adaptive Hub and adaptive button are turned on.
2. Press the pairing button on your adaptive button and wait for the light to flash.
3. On your Adaptive Hub, press and hold the pairing button until the light starts flashing.
4. The lights on both devices continue flashing. When the lights stop flashing, your adaptive button has successfully paired to the Adaptive Hub.

The button topper on all adaptive buttons is removable, allowing for maximum flexibility. To remove the button topper, do the following:

1. Secure the base of your adaptive button and place a hand on the button topper.
2. Twist the button topper counterclockwise to loosen it from the base until you can no longer turn it.
3. Remove the button topper from the button. One way to do this is to turn the button upside down.
4. Place the replacement button topper on the top of the adaptive button so the location markers match the base.
5. Twist the topper clockwise to secure it in place.

If you only want to change the joystick on the joystick button, here's how:

1. Secure the base of your adaptive button and place a hand on the joystick.
2. Twist the joystick counterclockwise to loosen it from the base.
3. Continue twisting it until you can pull the joystick away from the base.
4. Once you've removed the original joystick button topper, put the replacement joystick on the adaptive button.
5. Twist it clockwise to secure it in place.
