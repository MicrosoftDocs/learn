Before you capture a screenshot in your own work, consider whether a still image is the best way to communicate the information. A screenshot is most useful when the visual appearance or context of on-screen information matters and a still image captures what the recipient needs to understand.

## When a screenshot is a good fit

Consider using Snipping Tool when you want to:

- Show information as it appears on your screen.
- Capture the state of something at a particular moment.
- Focus someone's attention on a specific portion of on-screen content.
- Add visual emphasis to information before sharing it.

In these situations, a screenshot can provide visual context that might be difficult to communicate with text alone.

## When to consider another approach

A screenshot isn't always the most useful way to share information. Start with what your audience needs to do with the information.

- Consider sharing the original document, data, or a link to the source when your audience needs to explore, edit, or reuse the information. For example, if your Contoso colleagues need to examine the project data themselves instead of seeing a status snapshot, giving them access to the underlying dashboard might be more useful.
- Consider sharing text when the visual presentation isn't important. If your colleagues only need a project name and status, communicating those details directly might be simpler than capturing an image.
- Consider a recording or demonstration when your audience needs to see something change over time, such as the steps in a process. A still screenshot captures a moment, rather than showing a sequence of actions.

## Choose based on your audience

Before you reach for Snipping Tool, ask three questions:

- **Does the visual context matter?**
- **Does my audience need a snapshot, or do they need to interact with the source?**
- **Will a screenshot make the information easier to understand?**

For the Contoso project update, the answer points toward a screenshot. The layout of the dashboard provides useful context, your colleagues need a snapshot of the project's current state, and you can focus the image on the information that's relevant to them.

In another situation, those same questions might lead you to share the original content or a video recording instead.
