Hybrid intelligence makes the endpoint an active part of the AI computing environment. That shift makes the capabilities of the PC more important.

However, local AI isn't one workload. Different models and experiences can require different amounts and types of computing resources.

## Local AI workload requirements

The resources required for a local AI workload can depend on:

- Model size.
- Available memory.
- The type of inference.
- The required response characteristics.
- The frequency and duration of the workload.
- Whether other applications are using the same resources.
- The capabilities of the supporting runtime.

A device that supports one local AI experience might not be suited to every possible local AI workload.

For this reason, Contoso should think about PC capability in relation to workload requirements.

## Copilot+ PCs

Copilot+ PCs provide hardware [designed to support AI experiences on Windows](/collections/wpddbzt4p4zrow), including those that use local AI processing.

For everyday users, this local capability can make the PC a meaningful part of the hybrid-intelligence architecture. The endpoint isn't only a display and input device connected to cloud AI. It also contributes AI computing resources.

In the Contoso scenario, the hardware decision depends on which parts of the project-package workflow are expected to run locally. Contoso should identify the local models and processing required, how frequently employees will use them, and what other applications will compete for device resources.

A Copilot+ PC can provide a foundation for supported AI experiences that use local processing, but the appropriate configuration still depends on the workload.

## More demanding local workloads

Some users work with larger models or more computationally demanding local AI workloads. Developers, creators, engineers, and data professionals might require more local computing capacity than an everyday productivity experience.

These requirements can lead to systems with greater graphics, memory, or other computing capabilities.

At the upper end, specialized systems can provide significantly greater local AI capacity for engineering, research, or shared inference workloads. In short, hybrid intelligence spans a range of local-compute requirements.

## Avoid a one-size-fits-all approach

Contoso has multiple types of AI needs.

The employee preparing a project package represents an everyday productivity scenario. Another Contoso team might develop AI applications or work with more demanding local models.

Contoso can evaluate each role or use case against the practical requirements established in the scenario:

- Does the experience need to run a model locally?
- What resources does that model require?
- Is the workload occasional or sustained?
- Is the PC intended for everyday productivity or specialized AI work?
- Which capabilities can continue to use cloud intelligence?

These questions reflect the hybrid nature of the architecture. Contoso doesn't need to force every workload onto the endpoint. It can consider how endpoint and cloud resources complement each other.

## Key takeaways

Contoso began with a specific business outcome: help an employee turn selected project materials into a concise brief, a task list, and an organized set of source files. Delivering that outcome requires Contoso to consider where information and processing should reside, which local and cloud capabilities are needed, what resources an agent can access, and which PC hardware can support the selected local workloads.

That request now illustrates a broader architecture:

- **Hybrid intelligence** brings local and cloud AI resources together.
- **Intelligent routing** coordinates resources across the environments.
- **PC context, local actions, and local models** can contribute to richer Copilot experiences.
- **Containment, identity, and manageability** help control agentic work.
- **Capable PC hardware** determines which AI workloads can practically run locally.

Contoso doesn't need to view the PC and cloud as competing AI platforms. They can operate as complementary parts of one computing environment.
