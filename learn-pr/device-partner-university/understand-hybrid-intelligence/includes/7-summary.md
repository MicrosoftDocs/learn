Contoso began with a straightforward goal: give employees more capable AI experiences on their Windows PCs while maintaining appropriate organizational control. By exploring that scenario, you learned how hybrid intelligence changes the role of the PC as part of a broader AI computing environment.

In this module, you learned how to:

- **Describe how hybrid intelligence combines local and cloud AI on Windows.** Local computing and cloud intelligence can serve as complementary resources, with routing coordinating how those resources contribute to an AI experience.
- **Explain how local AI capabilities can contribute to Copilot experiences.** PC context, local actions, and local models help illustrate how capabilities on the PC can contribute alongside cloud intelligence.
- **Identify security and governance concepts that support agentic workloads.** Containment, identity, and manageability provide distinct concepts for controlling resource access, attributing agent activity, and applying organizational policy.
- **Explain the role of PC hardware in enabling local AI workloads.** Different local AI workloads can place different demands on PC computing resources, making endpoint capability an important consideration in a hybrid-intelligence architecture.

For Contoso, these concepts provide a way to think about the original challenge as a connected system rather than a collection of separate AI features. The employee can focus on preparing the project package, while the underlying AI experience can draw on local and cloud resources within the appropriate security boundaries.

Hybrid intelligence therefore represents a shift in the role of the Windows PC: the PC becomes part of the AI computing environment alongside the cloud.
