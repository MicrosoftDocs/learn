AI experiences are changing how people interact with their PCs. They can help people find information, generate content, and complete work. Increasingly, AI experiences can also use local computing resources and take action on a person's behalf.

This shift introduces several important questions:

- Does every AI operation need to run in the cloud?
- What can happen when AI models also run locally on a PC?
- How can local and cloud resources contribute to the same experience?
- How can an organization maintain appropriate control when an AI agent can take actions?
- What hardware is needed to support different local AI workloads?

To explore these questions, consider the following scenario.

## Contoso's AI challenge

Contoso is expanding its use of AI. The company wants to take greater advantage of the computing resources available on employees' Windows PCs while continuing to use cloud intelligence.

For example, an employee at Contoso needs to turn locally stored project documents, meeting notes, and supporting information into a project package for colleagues. The completed package should include a concise project brief, a task list, and an organized set of source files. The employee wants Copilot to help review the materials, identify relevant information, draft the brief, organize the files, and prepare the package for sharing.

The employee is interested in the outcome, not the underlying compute architecture. The employee shouldn't have to decide where every AI operation runs.

Contoso's IT team takes a broader perspective. Before supporting this experience, the team needs to consider several practical questions:

- Which project materials and processing tasks can remain on the employee's PC?
- Which parts of the experience require cloud-hosted models, services, or additional computing capacity?
- Which files, applications, and actions should the AI experience be allowed to access?
- How can Contoso distinguish and manage actions performed by an agent?
- What PC hardware is required to run the selected local AI workloads effectively?

These questions shape how Contoso combines local and cloud intelligence, establishes security boundaries, and selects appropriate PCs for different employees and workloads.

In this module, you explore the concepts that help answer that question. You'll examine hybrid intelligence, local AI in Copilot experiences, security for agentic work, and the relationship between local AI workloads and PC hardware.

## Learning objectives

After completing this module, you can:

- Describe how hybrid intelligence combines local and cloud AI on Windows.
- Explain how local AI capabilities can contribute to Copilot experiences.
- Identify security and governance concepts that support agentic workloads.
- Explain the role of PC hardware in enabling local AI workloads.
