Surface for Business devices include hardware experiences that can help reduce friction for users with different mobility, vision, and input needs. In the workplace, a barrier may look like inconsistent touchpad input, difficulty seeing key legends, or uncertainty about whether an action was completed. Inclusive hardware helps address those barriers by giving users more ways to interact with their device and confirm what they're doing.

Consider a project coordinator who prepares status updates across Word, Outlook, and Teams throughout the day. If standard touchpad gestures are difficult to perform consistently, Adaptive touch mode can help the coordinator navigate with a preferred input method. Haptic feedback can confirm actions such as selecting content or arranging windows, while larger, high-contrast key legends can make typing and shortcut use easier to verify at a glance.

On supported Surface devices, haptic touchpads use software-driven tactile feedback to make touchpad interactions feel more consistent across the touchpad area. This feedback can help users confirm select, taps, and supported Windows interactions without relying only on visual cues. Where available, haptics can also support contextual signals in Windows, such as confirmation during actions like snapping windows, aligning objects, or completing drag-and-drop. The workplace outcome is greater confidence: users can navigate, select, drag, and align content with more predictable feedback during everyday work.

>[!NOTE]
> Feature availability can vary by Surface device, keyboard, configuration, and region. Review the specifications for the device or accessory being used to confirm which inclusive hardware features are supported.
