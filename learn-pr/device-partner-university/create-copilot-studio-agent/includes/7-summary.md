## Key takeaways

In this module, you learned how to:

- **Plan an agent** by defining its purpose, audience, scope, approved knowledge, expected behavior, escalation approach, and validation criteria.

- **Test and refine an agent** by submitting representative requests, comparing responses with validation criteria, and updating the configuration when needed.

## Remember

An effective agent doesn't begin with configuration. It begins with a clear understanding of the problem it needs to solve, the audience it serves, the knowledge it can use, and the situations that require human assistance.

The planning brief serves as the foundation for the agent's instructions, knowledge, testing approach, and ongoing refinement. Whether you're creating an IT support agent, a customer service agent, or another type of business agent, the same planning process helps ensure that the agent remains focused on its intended purpose.

Without an agent, employees might need to locate and search support information themselves or contact the IT help desk for routine questions. Support team members would need to repeatedly direct employees to existing information while also handling issues that require investigation or technical assistance.

A well-defined agent can make approved support information easier to access, provide more consistent responses, and help users determine when they need human assistance. By validating and testing the agent before making it available to users, an organization can identify gaps, refine its behavior, and provide a more consistent support experience while allowing support teams to focus on issues that require direct assistance.
