AI is moving beyond experiences that merely generate content or answer questions. Instead of only responding to a user’s request, increasingly capable AI systems can participate in carrying out work. They can connect to data, use tools, interact with applications, and perform multi-step tasks on behalf of users.

Microsoft describes this shift as an agentic moment—a point where AI systems are becoming capable of moving beyond generating responses to taking actions and carrying out tasks across connected systems.

These capabilities can help organizations expand what users and teams can accomplish. They also change how organizations need to think about governance. While foundational AI governance focuses on managing AI systems and their outputs, agentic AI introduces additional questions about what systems can access, what actions they can take, how authority is delegated, and how those activities are monitored.

When an AI system can access information, use tools, make decisions, or take action, evaluating the quality of its responses is only part of the governance challenge. Organizations also need to understand what the system can access, what it's allowed to do, where human judgment is required, and how its behavior will be monitored over time.

As AI systems become more capable and interconnected, Microsoft is adapting its governance approach to address risks across the AI technology stack and throughout the AI lifecycle.

In this module, you'll explore how governance evolves as AI becomes more capable and learn how to evaluate governance requirements for AI deployments. You'll also examine how Microsoft approaches responsible AI governance and apply these concepts to an AI agent deployment scenario.
