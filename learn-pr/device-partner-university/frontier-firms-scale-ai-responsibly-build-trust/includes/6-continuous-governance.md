Responsible AI governance is not a fixed checklist, and the deployment recommendation you made in the previous unit is not the end of the governance process. As AI capabilities, deployments, and risks change, governance practices may need to evolve as well. Effective governance continues throughout the AI system's lifecycle, helping organizations adapt to new information, new capabilities, and changing risks.

## Use evidence to revisit governance decisions

Throughout this module, you've evaluated governance decisions based on an AI system's capabilities and authority. Those decisions shouldn't remain static. Information gathered through testing, user feedback, monitoring, incidents, and operational experience can help organizations determine whether existing safeguards, oversight requirements, permissions, and controls remain appropriate.

Organizations can use this evidence to revisit governance decisions as systems evolve, new capabilities are introduced, or risks change.

## Reassess capability and authority over time

Governance requirements can change whenever an AI system's capabilities or authority change.

As systems gain access to new data, connect to additional tools, support new users, or perform new actions, organizations should reassess whether existing safeguards, oversight mechanisms, and controls remain appropriate.

Ask periodically:

- Has the system gained new capabilities?
- Has its authority changed through new permissions, tools, or actions?
- Has access to data or systems changed?
- Are existing safeguards still effective?
- Have new risks emerged?
- Do governance controls still align with the system's current capability and authority?

This doesn't mean restarting the entire governance process every time something changes. Instead, organizations can use changes in capability, authority, or risk as signals to reassess the governance decisions that may be affected.

## Build trust through evidence

Trust in AI is built through demonstrated governance practices, not simply claims that a system is responsible. Organizations can build confidence by establishing practices that make governance observable and repeatable.

This can include:

- Documenting intended uses and limitations.
- Evaluating risks and mitigations.
- Establishing clear responsibilities.
- Testing systems before deployment.
- Applying appropriate safeguards.
- Monitoring systems after deployment.
- Recording and responding to incidents.
- Revisiting governance decisions as systems change.

These practices help turn responsible AI from a policy statement into an operational capability.

## Key takeaway

Effective AI governance evolves with the systems it governs. As an AI system's capabilities and authority change over time, organizations should reassess the safeguards, oversight, and controls that govern its use. Trust is built not through a one-time review, but through governance practices that continuously adapt to real-world experience.
