AI is moving from systems that primarily generate information toward systems that can interact with data, tools, applications, and users. As AI systems become more capable and agentic, governance needs to evolve beyond evaluating model outputs.

In this module, you learned how the rise of agentic AI changes governance requirements and why governance must account for the broader system rather than the model alone. You explored how organizations can assess AI deployments using a risk-based approach, evaluate access and authority, determine appropriate oversight and safeguards, and govern AI systems throughout their lifecycle. You also learned why monitoring and continuous reassessment are essential as AI systems, capabilities, and risks evolve.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Key takeaways**
>
> At this point in the module, you should be able to answer "yes" to all the following statements:
>
>- I understand why increasingly capable and agentic AI systems require governance beyond evaluating model outputs.
>- I can identify governance considerations related to an AI system's access, authority, oversight, security, and monitoring.
>- I understand how governance requirements can vary based on an AI system's capabilities, intended use, and potential impact.
>- I understand why governance should continue throughout the AI lifecycle rather than ending at deployment.
>- I can apply a governance-focused approach when evaluating a proposed AI deployment.

Frontier Firms can scale AI more responsibly by treating governance as an ongoing capability rather than a one-time approval step. Microsoft uses the term Frontier Firm to describe an organization that combines human judgment with AI and agents to transform business processes and ways of working. As AI systems become more capable and agentic, organizations that understand what AI systems can do, establish appropriate safeguards, and adapt governance over time will be better positioned to realize the benefits of AI while managing risk and maintaining trust.
