Organizations can build on safeguards provided by AI technology providers, but those safeguards are only one layer of an effective governance approach. Providers like Microsoft build security and responsible AI safeguards into the technologies they develop. Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard is one example. It establishes requirements for Microsoft's teams as they develop and deploy AI technologies, helping build security and responsible AI practices into the technology organizations use. These safeguards help address risks associated with the technology itself and provide capabilities that organizations can incorporate into their own security and governance approach.

| Responsible AI principle | How the principle helps govern AI systems |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| Fairness | AI systems are designed to help reduce bias and support equitable outcomes across different groups of users. |
| Reliability and Safety | AI systems are tested and evaluated to help ensure they operate as intended and remain safe under a variety of conditions. |
| Privacy and Security | Privacy protections and security controls are built into AI systems to help protect data and support compliance requirements. |
| Inclusiveness | AI systems are developed to support users with different abilities, needs, and backgrounds. |
| Transparency | Documentation and other resources help users understand how AI systems operate and what their capabilities and limitations are. |
| Accountability | Human oversight and governance mechanisms help ensure users remain responsible for decisions involving AI systems. |

While providers establish a secure and responsible foundation, organizations remain responsible for governing how AI systems are configured, deployed, and used within their environment. They need to consider the risks of their specific use case and determine how the AI should be configured and implemented, including what data it can access, what actions it can perform, and when human oversight is needed.

## Key takeaways

AI technologies can include security and responsible AI safeguards built in by the provider. Governance is a shared responsibility: providers secure and govern the technology they develop, while organizations remain responsible for governing how that technology is configured and used in their own environment. They need to govern their specific deployment, including its data access, permissions, actions, and human oversight.
