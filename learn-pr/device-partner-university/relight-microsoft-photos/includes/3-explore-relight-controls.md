You already decided on the photo you want to use for the big event. The composition is strong, your expression is professional, and the background works well. However, uneven lighting creates shadows that make the image look less polished than you intended.

Now it's time to explore how Relight can help you improve the image.

## Open your photo in Microsoft Photos

:::image type="content" border="true" source="../media/relight-interface.png" alt-text="Screenshot showing the interface for Relight in Microsoft Photos, with emphasis added to the lighting adjustment section." lightbox="../media/relight-interface.png":::

Begin by opening your portrait photo in Microsoft Photos on a Copilot+ PC and accessing the editing experience.

As you review the image, think about the areas that could benefit from better lighting. In this scenario, the goal isn't to dramatically change the photo. Instead, you want to create a more polished and professional appearance while maintaining a natural look.

## Reposition virtual light sources

One of the primary controls in Relight allows you to reposition virtual light sources around your subject.

As you move the light source, notice how different positions affect the portrait. You can:

- Reduce distracting shadows.
- Improve visibility of facial features.
- Create better balance across the image.
- Draw attention to the subject.

For your portrait, the most effective lighting position is often the one that improves facial visibility while retaining a natural, non-distracting appearance.

## Adjust light settings

Once you've identified a suitable light position, you can adjust the brightness and softness of the effect.

**Higher brightness** can brighten darker areas of the image, while **lower brightness** can produce a more subtle enhancement. Similarly, **higher softness** creates more diffused light, while **lower softness** creates a harder, more direct light source.

As you make adjustments, consider the purpose of the photo. Because you'll use this image in professional settings, you may wish to prioritize visual clarity and authenticity over stylistic flair.

## Review your changes as you work

Effective photo editing is often an iterative process.

As you experiment with different lighting positions and settings, compare the edited image with the original photo.

Ask yourself:

- Is your face easier to see?
- Does the image look balanced?
- Does the lighting appear realistic?
- Does the photo maintain a professional appearance?

These comparisons can help you determine whether each adjustment improves the image or introduces unnecessary changes.

## Key takeaways

In this unit, you learned that:

- Repositioning virtual light sources can help reduce shadows, improve facial visibility, and create better balance within a portrait.
- Adjusting brightness and softness allows you to refine the effect and achieve a natural-looking result that fits the intended use of the photo.
- Comparing an edited image with the original can help you evaluate whether lighting adjustments improve the overall appearance.
