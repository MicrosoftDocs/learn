Your event profile is nearly complete, but your updated photo is still missing. You have already selected a portrait with the right composition and expression. Now you need to address its uneven lighting and prepare it for professional use.

In this exercise, you'll use Relight to move from an existing photo to a finished portrait. Rather than trying to produce the strongest possible lighting effect, you'll make deliberate adjustments and validate that each one supports the photo's intended purpose.

>[!NOTE]
> Relight is available on Copilot+ PCs. Experiences can vary by device, market, and language and may require updates.

## Before you begin

You need:

- A Copilot+ PC
- Microsoft Photos
- A professional portrait photo with lighting you want to improve

Choose a photo in which the subject, expression, background, and composition already work well. Relight is most useful here because lighting is the primary issue you need to address.

## 1. Assess the original photo

Open your original portrait photo in Microsoft Photos. Before making any changes, identify the lighting problem you want to solve.

Look for a specific issue, such as:

- One side of your face being harder to see than the other.
- Shadows making facial features less visible.
- Uneven lighting pulling attention away from the subject.
- Insufficient separation between you and the background.

Choose one issue to address as your main editing goal. This gives you a clear basis for evaluating the result later.

## 2. Open Relight

Open the photo-editing experience and press **Relight**.

Take a moment to become familiar with the available lighting controls. You'll use them to test different lighting positions and refine the brightness and softness of the effect.

## 3. Find a useful lighting direction

Move a virtual light source around the photo and watch how the placement changes the image.

Try more than one position. As you experiment, focus on how the lighting affects:

- Facial visibility
- Shadow placement
- Balance across the portrait
- Attention on the subject

Choose the position that best addresses the lighting issue you identified in the first step. A useful position should improve visibility without drawing attention to the edit itself.

## 4. Refine the result

Adjust the brightness and softness to fine-tune your selected lighting position.

Begin with a restrained adjustment. Alter the settings until the subject is easier to see and the new lighting still fits naturally with the rest of the photo.

If the result feels too dramatic, reduce the brightness or increase the softness. Your goal is to strengthen the portrait, not substantially change how it looks.

## 5. Validate the edit

Compare the enhanced portrait with the original. Instead of looking only for a brighter image, check whether the edit solved the issue you identified at the beginning of the exercise.

Confirm that:

- The subject is easier to see.
- Distracting shadows have been reduced.
- The lighting looks balanced and believable.
- The photo remains recognizable and authentic.
- The image is appropriate for a customer-facing context.

If the edit doesn't meet these criteria, return to the lighting controls and make further adjustments.

## 6. Save the enhanced photo

When the portrait meets your goal, open **Save options** and select **Save as copy**. This creates a new version of the image while preserving the original photo, allowing you to return to the original or create additional variations later.

Your updated photo is now ready to use in event materials, workplace profiles, and other professional settings.

## Exercise outcome

You began with a portrait that already captured the professional image you wanted but had uneven lighting. By identifying a specific problem, testing a lighting direction, refining its settings, and validating the result, you created a more polished portrait for your upcoming customer-facing event.

## Reflection

Consider the decision that had the greatest effect on your final image:

- Choosing the position of the virtual light source
- Adjusting the light brightness and softness
- Comparing the enhanced photo with the original

How would you apply that decision when improving another professional photo?
