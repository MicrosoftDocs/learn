Your portrait is now ready for your upcoming customer-facing event. Along the way, you learned how to use Relight to refine an image by improving its lighting.

Knowing *how* to use Relight is useful. Knowing *when* to use it is equally important.

Not every photo needs editing, and not every issue can be solved with lighting adjustments. As you continue working with professional photos, you'll need to decide whether Relight is the right tool for the job.

This table can help you quickly make a decision. You will find more detailed criteria further down the page.

### Is Relight the right solution?

| **If the photo...** | **Use Relight?** | **Why?** |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Has uneven lighting or distracting shadows | ✅ Yes | Lighting is the primary issue, and Relight is designed to improve lighting. |
| Has good composition but the subject's face is hard to see | ✅ Yes | The photo is otherwise suitable and can benefit from lighting adjustments. |
| Is blurry or out of focus | ❌ No | Lighting changes won't improve image sharpness. |
| Has an awkward crop or framing | ❌ No | The issue isn't related to lighting. |
| Doesn't present the professional image you want | ❌ No | Selecting a different photo may be a better solution. |
| Needs major changes beyond lighting | ❌ No | Another editing tool or a different photo may be more appropriate. |

## Start by identifying the problem

Before making any edits, ask yourself a simple question:

**What is preventing this photo from being used as intended?**

In the scenario from this module, the answer was clear: the lighting wasn't ideal.

The photo already had:

- A professional expression
- Good composition
- An appropriate background
- Suitable image quality

Because lighting was the primary issue, Relight was a good solution.

## Situations where Relight can help

Relight is most effective when a portrait is already a strong photo but could benefit from improved lighting.

Consider using Relight when:

- Lighting falls unevenly across the subject.
- Shadows obscure facial features.
- An image needs minor refinements before being shared professionally.
- You want to improve an existing photo without recreating the original conditions.

In these situations, Relight can help improve visibility and balance while preserving the original image.

## Situations where another solution may be better

Relight improves lighting, but it doesn't address every issue that can affect a portrait.

You may want to choose a different photo or use a different photo editing solution when:

- The image is blurry.
- The subject is out of focus.
- The framing is awkward.
- Important details are cropped out.
- The expression doesn't fit the intended audience or context.
- The image doesn't represent how you want to present yourself professionally.

In these cases, selecting another photo or using a dedicated editing solution may lead to better results.

## Key takeaways

In this unit, you learned that:

- Relight is most useful when lighting is the primary issue affecting a portrait.
- Strong candidates for Relight already have good composition, image quality, and professional presentation.
- Some photo issues, such as blur, poor framing, or an unsuitable expression, can't be solved through lighting adjustments alone.
- Evaluating the underlying problem helps you determine whether Relight is the right tool for improving a professional portrait.
